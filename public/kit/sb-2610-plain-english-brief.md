## What this is (and is not)

This is a **short, plain-English orientation** for owners and managers who heard about Texas Senate Bill 2610 and want a starting map. It is **not** a substitute for reading the statute, legislative materials, or talking to qualified counsel.

**This is not legal advice.**

---

## In one paragraph

In the 89th Texas Legislature, **SB 2610** added provisions (Business & Commerce Code Chapter 542) about **cybersecurity programs** and **limits on certain civil liability** for qualifying **Texas business entities with fewer than 250 employees** that own or license computerized data including **sensitive personal information**. At a high level, the law is often described as creating a **safe-harbor style limitation on exemplary (punitive) damages** when a business can show it **implemented and maintained** a qualifying cybersecurity program **at the time of a breach** — not as a promise that you face “no liability,” and not as a marketing badge.

Effective date commonly cited from official legislative history: **September 1, 2025**. Always confirm against current official text.

---

## Why small employers care (practically)

1. **Size band matches many Texas SMBs** — applicability language focuses on entities with **fewer than 250 employees** (plus other conditions in the statute).
2. **Program expectations scale with size** — public summaries and bill text describe lighter expectations for very small teams and more structured expectations as headcount grows (including references to recognized frameworks / control sets for larger bands within that <250 range).
3. **Email and identity hygiene are still foundational** — whatever framework you choose, MFA on mail/admin, control of forwarding/rules, and a reporting path are practical building blocks. Tools like PhishGuard help **triage** suspicious URLs/email; they do not by themselves prove a statutory program.

---

## What you should *not* assume

- Do **not** assume “we bought a tool” or “we filled a scorecard” equals safe harbor.
- Do **not** assume the law removes all breach-related claims — public analyses emphasize limits on **exemplary / punitive** damages under stated conditions, not a blanket immunity.
- Do **not** treat blog posts or this brief as the statute.

---

## What to do next (reputation-safe)

1. Read the **enrolled bill text** and current statute posting from official Texas sources.
2. Ask counsel or a trusted advisor whether Chapter 542 applies to **your** entity and data types.
3. If you pursue a documented program, align controls to your size band and keep evidence of what you actually run (policies, MFA, training, reviews).
4. Use readiness worksheets (scorecard / MFA checklist) as **internal planning aids**, not as legal certifications.
